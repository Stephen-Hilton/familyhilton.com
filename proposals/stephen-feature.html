Stephen Hilton spent twenty years turning messy data into products. Then he went all-in on AI — and proved, solo, how fast one person can now move.

Stephen Hilton is a technical founder and solutions leader who's spent 20+ years turning messy data problems into products that ship — and the last several going all-in on AI. In early 2026 he built SkillSnap (skillsnap.me) solo, taking an AI résumé-and-cover-letter SaaS from an empty repo to revenue-positive in under eight weeks.

That sentence does a lot of quiet work. "Solo" means the AWS architecture, the AI pipeline, the billing, the pricing, the support flows — all of it. "Eight weeks" means he'd internalized the modern AI toolchain well enough that one person, working the way Hilton now works, can stand up a real business in the time it used to take to write a spec.

Concept to revenue-positive in under eight weeks — solo. Serverless AWS, a multi-model RAG pipeline, and the whole business around it. SkillSnap.me · early 2026

Underneath SkillSnap is a fully serverless AWS stack — Bedrock, Lambda, DynamoDB, SQS, S3, CloudFront, Cognito — and a multi-model generation pipeline that pairs LLMs with embedding-based retrieval (RAG) to produce tailored documents. Around it sits the actual company: Stripe billing, pricing, automated support. Building it, Hilton went deep on the modern AI toolchain — Claude Skills, MCP and RAG; orchestrating teams of Claude Code agents; AI-assisted development with Cursor.

None of which came from nowhere. Before SkillSnap, Hilton grew a pre-launch AI product from $0 to $3M ARR in 12 months and $14M in 24 at MakeInfinite Labs, where he also wrote the public docs, the first Rust CLI, and the Python SDK for an enterprise AI platform, and gave 20-plus conference talks ("Natural Language → SQL via AI" at EthDenver; "AI to ZK: New Data Pipelines" at Consensys). Before that he ran an $80M+ solution-engineering territory at Teradata — Apple, Netflix, Meta, LinkedIn, eBay, PayPal — and was building petabyte-scale Hadoop / Spark / Kafka platforms before "data engineering" was a job title.

He's hands-on in Python and Rust, holds an AWS Solutions Architect cert and an MBA, and is currently fractional CTO/CPO at a fintech startup — AWS serverless, a Rust + Postgres data platform, AI-agentic auto-healing infrastructure, five-nines transaction systems. He also runs their college intern program, which tells you something about how he likes to work.

§How I built SkillSnap

The short version: take it seriously as a business, not a demo — and let the AI toolchain compress the parts that used to take a team.

Field notes · concept → revenue, <8 weeks

SkillSnap, in eight moves

An AI résumé & cover-letter SaaS, built solo in early 2026. Here's how the pieces went together.

Pick a real problem with willingness to pay

Tailored résumés and cover letters — concrete output, obvious value, a buyer who'll pay on day one.

Go serverless on AWS from line one

Lambda for compute, DynamoDB for state, SQS for the work queue, S3 for documents, CloudFront for delivery, Cognito for auth. Nothing to babysit; scales to zero.

Build the generation pipeline on Bedrock

A multi-model approach — different models for different jobs in the chain — instead of betting everything on one prompt.

Add retrieval (RAG) so output is grounded

Embedding-based retrieval pulls the right context — the user's history, the target role — so documents are tailored, not generic.

Wire up the money: Stripe

Billing, plans, pricing — a working revenue path, not a "contact sales" placeholder.

Automate support

Onboarding, common questions, edge cases handled without a human in the loop for routine flows.

Use the modern toolchain to move fast

Claude Skills, MCP and RAG; teams of Claude Code agents doing real work in parallel; Cursor for AI-assisted dev.

Ship, then iterate against revenue

Live and revenue-positive inside eight weeks — then let paying users set the roadmap.
