Thanks for opening this. I figured the most honest way to introduce myself wasn't a deck or a one-pager — it was a letter. So here's the real version: who I am, what I've built, what I'm chasing right now, and the six people and three animals who keep the rest of my life loud and good.

I'm a builder. For twenty-odd years that meant taking messy, sprawling data problems and turning them into things that actually ship — products, platforms, pipelines that people depend on. For the last several years it's meant going all-in on AI: not as a buzzword bolted onto a roadmap, but as the thing I get up early to work on.

The thing I'm proudest of right now

In early 2026 I built SkillSnap (skillsnap.me) — solo. Empty repo to revenue-positive in under eight weeks. It's an AI résumé-and-cover-letter SaaS, and I owned all of it: a fully serverless AWS stack — Bedrock, Lambda, DynamoDB, SQS, S3, CloudFront, Cognito — a multi-model generation pipeline that pairs LLMs with embedding-based retrieval (RAG) so the documents it produces are actually tailored, not generic mush. And the whole business around it: Stripe billing, the pricing, the automated support. Front to back, infra to invoice.

I wanted to know — really know — what it takes to ship an AI product alone in 2026. Now I do. It takes the toolchain, the taste to use it well, and the stubbornness to finish.

Building it pushed me deep into the modern AI toolchain: Claude Skills, MCP, and RAG; orchestrating teams of Claude Code agents like a small engineering crew; AI-assisted development with Cursor. I'm hands-on in Python, increasingly in Rust, and I hold an AWS Solutions Architect cert and an MBA — but honestly the cert that matters most this year is the one nobody issues: I sat down and built the whole thing.

The arc that got me here

Before SkillSnap I spent two years at MakeInfinite Labs taking a pre-launch AI product from $0 to $3M ARR in twelve months, and $14M in twenty-four — as Chief of Staff, then Head of Solutions / GTM, working with 900+ technical stakeholders, giving 20+ conference talks, and writing the platform's first Rust CLI and Python SDK and its public docs.

Before that, twelve years at Teradata: I ran an $80M+ solution-engineering territory across the Western US — Apple, Netflix, Meta, LinkedIn, eBay, PayPal, EA, Ubisoft, roughly half the US compute capacity — then moved into a global strategy role building a telemetry framework across 2,000+ accounts and ~$2B ARR, with reporting that went to the board. And before that — before "data engineering" was a job title — I was standing up petabyte-scale Hadoop, Spark and Kafka platforms, going back to a decade leading BI and the Innovation Center at Best Buy across 4,000+ stores.

I lead the way I build: close to the work, allergic to hand-waving, happiest when a thorny problem and a shippable answer are in the same room. Right now I'm also serving as fractional CTO / CPO at a stealth fintech — AWS serverless, a Rust + Postgres data platform, AI-agentic auto-healing infrastructure, five-nines transaction systems — and I run their college intern program, which is its own kind of fun.

What I'm into lately

Claude Skills and MCP and the whole agent-orchestration space — that's where my evenings go. Cursor as a daily driver. Rust, because it makes me a more careful programmer. And the through-line of my whole career: turning the firehose of data and models into something a person can actually use.

And then there's the loud, good part

We live in sunny Napa County — two adults, two teenagers, and one lazy golden retriever, Sunny, who is fairly sure she owns the rest of us. Joy, my wife, is a biologist and the Education Director at the American Canyon Community & Parks Foundation. One teen is deep in theater and K-pop; the other in ecology and biology; both are deep in D&D. Interests in this house run wide — AI and data, ecology, K-pop, theater, video games — and the one thing everyone signs off on is travel: at least one big trip a year, usually Europe (Scotland, France, Greece…), maybe Japan next.

I'm telling you this not as small talk but because it's load-bearing. The same person who'll obsess over a RAG pipeline at 6am is the one who packs five people and a route through three countries every summer. Same brain. I bring all of it.

If you're hiring

I'm open to senior AI, solutions, or platform roles — places where the gap between "interesting AI idea" and "thing customers pay for" is the actual job. That gap is where I've spent my whole career, and lately it's narrower and more exciting than it's ever been. If that's the shape of what you need, I'd love to talk.
