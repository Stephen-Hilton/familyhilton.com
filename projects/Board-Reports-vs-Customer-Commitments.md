# Overview

### Situation:
- Early-stage MakeInfinite Labs, constant tight deadlines and competing priorities
- Quarterly board/investor reports required, owned GTM + customer usage sections
- Environment highly dynamic; full data reconciliation performed quarterly for trust
- CFO owned financials; required reconciliation between contract ARR and actual ARR
- CEO often gave very short notice (few days) to deliver board deck
- We committed integration date to Weave.services, startup building Web3 version of n8n / Zapier
- Simultaneous commitments to critical customer projects created conflict

### Tactics:
- Work with CEO to clarified priorities and timelines
- Advocate for customer-first approach, with adjusted board report timeline
- Advocated longer-term improvements to planning, after critical issues resolved

### Actions:
- Texted CEO for actual board deck delivery date - Tuesday
- Scheduled 15min call to outline options and recommendation
- Trade-off options: 
    - Deliver deck on Friday, and miss customer deliverable
    - Deliver Deck on Monday PM, and deliver to customer (recommended)
- CEO agreed: allowed report review Monday evening/Tuesday AM
- Scheduled time with CFO to reconcile on Monday AM
- Delivered customer integration on time
- Completed board report on time, high quality maintained
- Absorbed personal cost (weekend work) to meet both deliverables
- Raised issue again during 1:1 with CEO, highlighting recurring conflict
- CEO improved planning over time, giving 2–3 weeks’ notice for board reports

### Results:
- Converted strategic prospect to paying customer
    - Their revenue was small, <1k /mo
    - But also converted 2nd hand customers, via their integration
- Delivered accurate, reconciled board reports on time
- Strengthened CEO trust through proactive recommendations
- Improved long-term CEO planning of board report prep (2–3 weeks’ notice)
- Maintained investor trust in data accuracy and quality
- Balanced customer and investor demands despite lean resources

Start-ups are nothing but tight deadlines and competing priorities. One of the most stressful at MakeInfinite was preparing the quarterly board of directors/investor report on the business, for which I owned the “go to market” and “customer usage” sections – two of the most important sections in the report. Additionally, while the CFO owned the financial section, he and I needed to reconcile “contract ARR” and “actual ARR” and be able to explain any delta. Early on, the tight timelines were self-inflicted; the CEO often gave us only a few days’ notice to reconcile and prepare the reports. Because I ran a lean GTM team, we always had some critical customer project underway. For example, one quarter we had committed to completing data integration for a large prospect, sprinting to meet their release on Thursday...

The strategy was simple: a quick call with the CEO to clarify priorities and timelines, followed by a recommendation. In this case, I texted the CEO asking for the actual date the report was being sent out, which was the following Tuesday. I then scheduled a quick 15-minute call with the CEO and outlined the two choices we had: deliver the quarterly report by Friday and miss on a customer commitment, or my recommendation, meet our customer commitments, postpone delivery of the quarterly deck until Sunday n...

The CEO agreed with my recommendation. We delivered to the prospect, who converted to a paying customer, and provided the board report on time, with its expected high quality. The trade-off was my loss of a Saturday and part of Sunday. This wasn’t ideal, risking burnout, but not unheard of in startups. The CEO agreed and improved over time, eventually scheduling board reports 2–3 weeks out.
