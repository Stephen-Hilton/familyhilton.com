# Overview

### Situation:
- Role: Head of GTM at MakeInfinite Labs, owning all enterprise accounts
- Supported SEs and sales across all accounts
- Communication scattered across Telegram, Signal, Slack, Discord, WhatsApp, SMS, email
- Email least-used except for enterprise accounts and meetings

### Tactics:
- Missed critical enterprise email during $6B account deal, delaying close by 1 week
- Recognized risk of communication overload causing dropped items
- Determined need to prioritize enterprise communications

### Actions:
- Blocked 30 mins, twice weekly, for enterprise follow-ups
- Reviewed last communications for each enterprise account, ensuring forward progress
- Elevated standards of engagement for strategic accounts
- Pushed smaller customers toward automated/onboarding paths
- Limited small-customer interactions to 30-min intro calls until paid services

### Results:
- Prevented further missed enterprise communications
- Maintained forward momentum on strategic deals
- Improved enterprise customer perception of responsiveness
- Balanced workload across 20+ active prospects
- Focused GTM resources where impact was highest

# Full Text
At MakeInfinite Labs as Head of GTM, I owned all enterprise accounts directly, and helped other SEs and sales with all accounts. One challenge was staying on top of the many communication channels: Telegram, Signal, Slack, Discord, WhatsApp, text messages, and email. Email was least used—except for enterprise customers.  

While closing a deal with a $6B enterprise, I missed a customer email. It wasn’t critical, but it delayed closing by about a week, hurting responsiveness. This highlighted we were juggling 20+ prospects and moving too fast. Dropping small start-ups was acceptable; dropping enterprise customers was not.  

To fix this, I blocked 30 minutes twice weekly for “enterprise follow-up.” I reviewed last communications with each enterprise account and ensured forward momentum. I also increased interaction standards, automating smaller customer paths and limiting them to 30-minute intro calls until they signed up.  

This simple discipline prevented further misses, ensured responsiveness for strategic accounts, and balanced GTM focus across large and small customers.
