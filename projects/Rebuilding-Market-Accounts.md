# Overview

### Situation:
- Director of SEs at Teradata, usually focused on enterprise customers
- Asked to lead Market Accounts SE team (SMB customers, Western US)
- Team struggling after turnover; many inexperienced, sales down
- SEs lacked clear guidance, confidence, and connection 

### Tactics:
- Transitioned enterprise customers
- Plan: assess fit/needs, mentor SEs, foster the team
- Work to build a culture of collaboration and mentorship

### Actions:
- Organized 3-day in-person HQ meeting:
  - Day 1: introductions, skills/experience sharing, I set expectations and shared resources
  - Day 2: product engineering sessions for learning and excitement (still have pic!)
  - Day 3: account planning and cross-account collaboration
  - had lots of fun, learned a lot, 100% more engaged team by end
- Homework: assigned cross-account backkups / redundancies
- Established monthly in-person tour across California regions
- Collected and resolved SE blockers, positioning self as lineman supporting SEs

### Results:
- Market Accounts achieved $22M sales vs $20M quota
- One of the new SEs won the "New SE of the year" across the whole company! 
- Transformed team into cohesive, collaborative whole
- Personal growth: learned to prioritize and say “Yes, if…” with 50+ customers

# Full Text
I spent several years at Teradata as Director of Solution Engineering, leading SE teams in California and Silicon Valley with the largest, most complex customers. I held a very high standard for SEs in my region.  

One year, I was asked to move over to Market Accounts, leading SEs working on SMB customers across California. The team had suffered turnover under the last director, and many inexperienced SEs struggled to engage customers. Sales were down, and confidence was low.  

After ensuring enterprise customers were covered, I devised a plan: (a) get to know the SEs, (b) assess needs/fit/skills, (c) build per-SE development plan on how to help them perform. I started with intro calls, then a 3-day in-person meeting at HQ. Day 1: introductions, skills sharing, expectations, collateral review. Day 2: product engineering sessions for connection and learning. Day 3: account planning and cross-support discussions. Meals and social time built camaraderie.  

I identified juniors needing help and seniors ready to mentor. I began a monthly statewide tour (Sacramento, SF, Anaheim, OC, SD), coaching SEs and removing blockers. SEs were quarterbacks; I was the lineman clearing obstacles.  

After one year, sales hit $22M vs $20M quota, and the team became a cohesive, confident group. Though I returned to enterprise accounts, I valued the Market Accounts year for growth: leading many SMBs taught me to prioritize and often say, “Yes, if…” rather than “No.”
