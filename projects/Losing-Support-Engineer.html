Losing Support Engineer & Protecting Customer Experience

Faced sudden loss of only customer support engineer at MakeInfinite Labs. Balanced need to protect Solution Engineers from burnout while ensuring global customer coverage. Aligned with Head of Engineering, opposed CEO’s RIF decision, but responsibility shifted to Eng. Result: slowed roadmap ≈5-10%, exposed engineering to customers.

Overview

Situation:

  • MakeInfinite Labs startup environment, limited resources and global customers
  • Support engineer was OK Eng., providing other support when no tickets
  • CEO looking for cost savings, asked to RIF only IST customer support engineer
  • Solution Engineers (SEs) under pressure to cover support, risking presales focus
  • Wasn't a named-person concern, but role and timezone issue: no support 12hs/day

Tactics:

  • Engaged Head of Engineering first, to align on united response
  • Advocate for delaying RIF until replacement secured

Actions:

  • Jointly aligned with Head of Eng. on risks of losing IST support
  • Scheduled call with CEO + Head of Eng. to present case
    • Raised concern to CEO about RIF decision
    • Urged retaining resource until replacement could be hired
    • Clarified value of low-cost, pre-trained support resource
  • CEO overruled, executed RIF, assigned support to Eng.

Results:

  • Roadmap progress slowed ≈5-10% due to Eng. handling support
  • Lost some of their most talented engineers, too stressful
  • Customer confusion from direct Eng. exposure
  • Despite outcome, GTM + Eng. remained well alignment under pressure

Customer Obsession:

Protecting SE productivity while raising support risks.

  • Ownership – Escalated support coverage gap, drove alignment across GTM and Eng.
  • Customer Obsession – Fought for global customer coverage for optimal customer experience
  • Dive Deep – Evaluated backlog, time zones, and impact of losing resource.
  • Earn Trust – Partnered with Head of Eng. to ensure unified message to CEO.
  • Disagree and Commit – Predicatbly roadmap slowed ≈5-10%, fought CEO even when overruled.

Full Text

Let’s talk about the time I lost my only customer support engineer, while trying to keep Solution Engineers (architects) focused on driving new business. Support at startups often falls to Solution Engineering, being a technical resource who is plugged into both customer needs as well as product engineering. That said, there comes a point when you need to split those functions, so you don’t burn out your SEs – especially when they’re all in the US time zone but have global customers looking for off-hour ... Because of ongoing support backlog and time zone challenges, I had escalated to engineering and they provided me one of their mediocre IST engineer resources. While they were an average developer, they were a fine support engineer, and they serviced customers during US time overnights, and handed off challenges to the engineering team, or to the solution engineering team, depending on the situation. However, the resource still technically reported to engineering, according to HR.

After a poor annual review by the engineering manager, the CEO decided he was going to RIF the resource, without any plan to replace the resource. This was obviously a huge problem, as it left our growing customer base without support for 12 hours a day, or required some US resources like a Solution Engineer to be page-able during overnights, which would obviously impact presales activities. I messaged the CEO to raise concerns on removing the position – noting that the current resource was low-cost, pre-t... I called the Head of Engineering and asked if this was in his plan, which it was not. The two of us talked through all the support challenges without replacing the resource, and got aligned on our message – both GTM and Eng. teams were at capacity, so pushing support to either team means lowering output of that team. We agreed Eng. made more sense, since (a) they were in a better position to fix, and (b) they had IST resources.

With a united message between GTM and Eng. locked down, I scheduled a call with myself, Head of Eng., and the CEO. We made the joint case why we shouldn’t RIF this employee until another support person could be hired. The CEO didn’t want to wait, and over both our objections had HR execute the RIF, pushing the responsibility to Eng.

Unsurprisingly, this slowed the roadmap by about ≈5-10%, as Eng. now had to spend time diagnosing issues and dealing with false-positive issues. It also exposed product engineering to customers directly, which caused confusion when questions came up during US time.
