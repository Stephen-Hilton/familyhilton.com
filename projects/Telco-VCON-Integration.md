# Overview

### Situation:
- Medium-sized telco ($740M revenue) providing secure video and text/MFA services
- High MFA costs (~10¢ per message) drove search for alternatives
- Interested in AI/ZK database and VCON storage for verifiable consumer ID

### Tactics:
- Telco slow-moving, risk-averse; startup needed to demonstrate credibility and ease-of-use
- We were web3 and media tech; needed to borrow some telco credibility

### Actions:
- Partnered with consultancy, supported with technical expertise, co-authored whitepaper
- Partnered with CTO of VCON framework company, built rapid v0.5 integration
    - Submitted PR to VCON-Server repo, merged into production framework
    - Demonstrated ability to extend backend with minimal effort
- Provided pro-bono system access during development phase
- Built credibility by aligning trusted partners and fast technical execution

### Results:
- Whitepaper published, boosting telco confidence in solution (and great PR)
- Rapid integration demonstrated product ease-of-use and maturity
- Project kicked off with $80K consulting billed to date
- Estimated potential throughput: 1.3B monthly messages once live
- Revenue potential: $3.5M–$7M per month from telco adoption

# Full Text
As a small tech startup, introductions to mature spaces like telco can be a mixed bag. On one hand, they have the funding to single-handedly make your startup succeed. On the other, they move extremely slow, requiring a large investment of time before they’ll purchase.  

We had such an opportunity with a medium-sized telco provider of voice services ($740M revenue) delivering secure video meetings and MFA text services. They were interested in our AI/ZK database as a verifiable MFA alternative. MFA texts cost ~10¢, a significant cost given millions sent monthly.  

Central to the project was storing VCONs (virtual conversations), append-only structures consumable by AI. These allowed agents to analyze, act, and record output verifiably. Our AI + ZK-enabled platform was a natural fit for VCON storage.  

To balance telco caution with startup agility, we engaged two trusted partners:  
- A consultancy known to the telco, who co-authored an industry whitepaper with me.  
- The CTO of the VCON framework company, where I contributed a v0.5 backend integration PR into VCON-Server.  

The consultancy provided domain trust; I partnered with them technically and co-authored the Cavell whitepaper:  
https://www.cavell.com/unlocking-the-value-of-conversational-data-virtualized-conversations-vcon-and-the-future-of-communication/  

For VCON-Server, I built a backend integration with Space and Time, submitting a PR that was merged:  
https://github.com/vcon-dev/vcon-server/tree/842e36e651b6922657e73601fc9e2d4101b09417/server/storage/spaceandtime  

This one-two punch of domain knowledge and rapid integration proved our product’s credibility and usability. We agreed to provide pro-bono access during development. The consulting team billed $80K so far; projected production use is 1.3B monthly messages, generating $3.5M–$7M in revenue.
