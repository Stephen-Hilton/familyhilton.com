Full Text

At MakeInfinite Labs, one of the founders—let’s call him F—developed a reputation for being extremely volatile. He regularly came into meetings angry, emotional, and combative, wielding his ego in ways that often left employees in tears or deeply frustrated. Over time, the behavior escalated. HR received multiple complaints, but because F was a co-owner, there was little they could do.

The problem soon began affecting the business. I lost my second-best Solution Engineer, and shortly afterward, my top SE was on the verge of leaving as well. Retention was becoming a real issue, and the team’s morale was deteriorating.

To protect my people, I began adjusting how we operated. I instructed the team not to engage with F directly, but instead to route all interactions through me so I could shield them. Personally, I minimized my own contact with F by working more with his lieutenants. I also raised concerns with the CEO multiple times, but the situation only worsened. To make matters worse, younger leaders in the company began imitating F’s aggressive style, thinking it was the accepted way to lead.

Finally, I escalated the issue directly with the CEO, stressing the severity: we were bleeding talent, our exposure to legal risk was rising, and if I left, my entire team would likely follow me. That conversation appeared to be the turning point.

I don’t know exactly what steps the CEO took, though I suspect he raised the matter with the board of directors, which included the founders, and may have pressured F to seek coaching. What I do know is that F disappeared for a couple of weeks on PTO. When he returned, his behavior was noticeably different. He was suddenly much more polite and began using phrases that sounded learned, like “I’m frustrated because…” and “I appreciate all of your work…” While the sincerity was questionable, the change was nonetheless positive.

The shift stemmed the loss of employees and began improving the company’s culture. While far from perfect, the reduced hostility allowed the team to stabilize, focus on their work, and start rebuilding trust in the leadership environment.
