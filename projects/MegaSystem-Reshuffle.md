# Full Text
Because of my location in the San Francisco Bay/Silicon Valley area, my region was the largest in the company—50% of all compute in North America, which was itself 50% of global compute. One customer accounted for more than half my region’s compute (1/8 of global total): a $400B revenue, $3.5T market cap consumer hardware/software giant.  

Deals there were always large and complex. We worked with the customer on multi-year roadmaps, closing $40M–$50M deals annually. Increasing commoditization of hardware added complexity, often involving reshuffling five systems across multiple data centers.  

In my last year in this role, the customer requested a major reshuffle to support new use cases and add a new system. Support worried about risks from timeline, hardware age, and scale. Sales pushed for urgency with a $42M contract pending.  

I directly led SEs and CSMs. We finalized the customer’s HW movement map, then held twice-weekly calls with Sales, Support, and SEs. Support flagged insufficient power capacity in one hall, which I raised with the customer. They appreciated our awareness, and we collaboratively adjusted.  

Within three weeks, we gained approval internally and externally. We closed the $42M deal and executed the plan. The only hiccup: our suppliers ran out of 200mbps infiniband cables. Support resolved it by reallocating, raiding parts stores, and ordering replacements. Install completed successfully, delivering new use cases for our largest global client.
